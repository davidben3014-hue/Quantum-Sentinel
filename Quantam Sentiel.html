The ground beneath critical infrastructure is complex. Quantum Sentinel proposes combining quantum gravity sensing, analytical models and digital mapping to make subtle underground variations easier to examine.

01 / The mission

A clearer view of
what lies below.

Quantum Sentinel is an early-stage proposal for a quantum-powered underground monitoring system—not a deployed sensor or completed monitoring platform.

The problem

Infrastructure has a hidden dimension.

Underground conditions can change in ways that are difficult to assess from the surface alone. Excavation and conventional surveys can be resource-intensive, and no single measurement tells the whole story.

What if tiny gravity variations could help guide where experts look next?

Infrastructure / the unseen environment below
The proposed solution

From measurement
to meaningful questions.

01 / SENSE

Measure carefully

Explore whether quantum gravity measurements could capture small spatial changes in gravitational acceleration.

02 / INTERPRET

Find patterns

Compare measurements, account for uncertainty and use computational analysis to flag unusual patterns.

03 / INVESTIGATE

Support decisions

Visualise areas of interest so qualified engineers can decide where further surveying may be useful.

Core objectives
01 Research the principles and limits of quantum gravity sensing.
02 Design a plausible end-to-end monitoring workflow.
03 Build transparent simulations and visualisations.
04 Define validation steps before any real-world claims.
Important: A gravity anomaly does not directly confirm a water leak, locate a specific asset, or identify an exact underground material. Multiple explanations are possible and require independent investigation.

06 / Evidence base

Grounded in
real research.

The concept draws on published quantum sensing research. These sources support the underlying physical methods; they do not validate Quantum Sentinel as a finished system.

Selected references / open in new tab
Peer-reviewed research · Nature · 2022

Quantum sensing for gravity cartography

B. Stray et al. Nature 602, 590–594 (2022). DOI: 10.1038/s41586-021-04315-3. Demonstrates an atom-interferometry gravity gradiometer detecting a buried tunnel in an urban survey.
Foundational review · Reviews of Modern Physics · 2009

Atom interferometry

A. D. Cronin, J. Schmiedmayer and D. E. Pritchard. Reviews of Modern Physics 81, 1051–1129 (2009). DOI: 10.1103/RevModPhys.81.1051. A foundational review of atom-interferometry principles.
Foundational experiment · Physical Review Letters · 1991

Measurement of the Earth’s gravity by an atomic interferometer

M. Kasevich and S. Chu. Physical Review Letters 67, 181–184 (1991). DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevLett.67.181. An early demonstration of measuring gravity using atom interferometry.
Research interpretation: Detecting a gravity variation is different from determining its unique cause. Ground truth, environmental corrections and independent survey methods are essential to practical evaluation.

08 / Development pathway

A concept with

Quantum Sentinel is currently at Technology Readiness Level 2: the technology concept and possible application have been formulated. Future milestones are proposed, not completed.

Current maturity / proposed concept

Scientific principles have been identified and an application concept is being developed. No completed quantum sensing system or field validation is claimed.
